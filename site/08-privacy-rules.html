08. Privacy Rules: What Never to Share

Free AI tools are helpful, but anything you type might be seen by the company that runs the tool, used to train future models, or stored in logs. Treat an AI chat like a public conversation — helpful, but not private.

The golden rule

If you wouldn't write it on a postcard and mail it to a stranger, don't type it into an AI chat.

Never share with an AI

  • Full name, home address, phone number, email address
  • Social Security number, driver's license, passport numbers
  • Bank account numbers, credit card numbers, PINs, passwords
  • Dates of birth (especially yours or your family's)
  • Medical diagnoses, test results, medication lists, health details about real people
  • Employer details, pay stubs, tax returns, customer lists, business secrets
  • Children's names, schools, or schedules
  • Exact location details ("I live at...")
  • Passwords or answers to security questions

What's okay to share (general, non-personal)

  • "I have chicken, rice, and broccoli" (no names, no address)
  • "I cook for two adults on a $60 weekly budget"
  • "I need gluten-free dinners" (your restriction type, not your medical record)
  • "I have a slow cooker and a microwave"

Meal-planning specifics

It's safe to tell an AI:

  • Your ingredient list
  • General dietary preferences (vegetarian, low-sodium, diabetes-friendly as a category)
  • Your rough budget
  • How many people you cook for

It's risky to tell an AI:

  • "My husband John has diabetes and takes insulin" — that's someone else's health info
  • "I shop at the [store] on Main Street at 53rd" — that's your location
  • "My credit card ends in 4422" — never share payment info

Practical privacy habits

  1. Start a new chat for each meal-planning project — don't keep one long chat with months of personal details in it.
  2. Delete chats you no longer need — most tools let you delete conversation history.
  3. Use guest or temporary modes if the tool offers them.
  4. Read the tool's privacy policy — look for "we may use your input to train our models." If you don't like that, choose a different tool.
  5. Opt out of training if the tool allows it (settings → data controls).

If you already shared something

Don't panic. Most likely nothing bad will happen. Just don't share more, delete the chat if you can, and be more careful next time. If you shared a password, change it.

Medical privacy note

This guide is education only. AI is not covered by HIPAA (the U.S. health privacy law). Never share real patient information, real medical records, or identifiable health details about anyone — including yourself — with an AI chat tool.

Try this

Before your next AI chat, ask yourself: "Is there anything in this message I wouldn't want a stranger to read?" If yes, take it out or make it generic ("a family member" instead of a name).
