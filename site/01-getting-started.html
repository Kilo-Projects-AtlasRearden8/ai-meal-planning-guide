01. Getting Started with Free AI

You don't need to buy anything to plan meals with AI. A free AI chat tool is enough.

Step 1: Pick a free AI chat tool

Good free options (as of this writing):

  • Google Gemini — gemini.google.com (free tier)
  • Anthropic Claude — claude.ai (free tier)
  • Microsoft Copilot — copilot.microsoft.com (free tier)
  • Perplexity — perplexity.ai (free tier, good for recipe research)
  • Meta AI — meta.ai (free where available)

You only need one. Pick whichever is easiest for you to reach on your phone or computer. All of them let you type a question and get an answer back.

Step 2: Create an account (usually just an email)

Most free tools ask for an email address to sign in. That's normal. You do not need to give your address, phone number, income, or health information to use the tool. See Section 08 for what never to share.

Step 3: Start a new chat

Click "New chat" or "New conversation." Type something simple like:

I want to plan dinners for the week. What do you need to know from me?

The AI will probably ask what ingredients you have, how many people you're cooking for, your budget, and any dietary restrictions. That's exactly what you want.

Step 4: Talk back and forth

AI meal planning works best as a conversation, not a one-shot command. Tell it what you have, ask for ideas, ask it to revise, ask for a shopping list, repeat.

Example first message

Hi. I want to plan 5 dinners for 2 adults. I have: chicken thighs, rice, canned black beans, frozen broccoli, eggs, pasta, a jar of marinara, onions, and garlic. I spend about $60 a week on groceries. Can you suggest a menu and a grocery list? I'd like simple recipes, under 30 minutes each.

Why free AI works for meal planning

  • It knows thousands of ingredient combinations and flavor pairings
  • It can turn a random list of pantry items into recipe ideas
  • It can scale recipes up or down for your household size
  • It can build a grocery list organized by store section
  • It remembers what you told it within the same chat

Limitations to keep in mind

  • Free AI can invent facts (called "hallucinations"). Always double-check cooking times and food-safety details.
  • It doesn't know what's actually in your fridge unless you tell it.
  • It can't see prices at your local store.
  • It is not a dietitian or doctor.

Try this

Copy and paste this into a new chat:

I'm new to using AI for meal planning. Please walk me through, step by step, how to give you a list of ingredients I have at home and get back a weekly dinner menu with a grocery list. Keep your answers short and beginner-friendly.

Then reply with your actual pantry list and see what happens.
