# Introduction: AI Meal Planning, Plain English

Welcome. This guide shows you how to use free AI chat tools to plan a week of meals, starting from whatever you already have in your pantry and fridge. You don't need to pay for anything, you don't need to be a "tech person," and you don't need to share anything private.

Written by Dylan Asdale, founder of **Asdale Dynamics, LLC** — beginner-friendly AI education, books, and practical tools. His book *The Massive AI Manual* and hub site dylanasdale.github.io explain AI "like a friend would": plain English, step by step, never condescending.

## Who this is for

- Anyone who stares into the fridge and wonders, "What can I make with this?"
- People on a grocery budget who want to waste less food
- Cooks juggling dietary restrictions (gluten-free, dairy-free, vegetarian, diabetes-friendly, low-sodium, and more)
- Beginners who have never used an AI chat tool before
- Nurses, shift workers, and busy folks who want low-energy, low-stress meal planning

## What you'll learn

1. How to pick a free AI chat tool and start a conversation
2. How to turn pantry ingredients into a full weekly menu
3. How to build a grocery list from that menu
4. How to adjust for dietary restrictions and budgets
5. Copy-and-paste prompts that actually work
6. Printable templates you can fill in by hand
7. Privacy rules: what never to share with an AI

## How to use this guide

Read it straight through, or jump to the section you need right now. Every section has a "Try this" box with a prompt you can copy and paste into an AI chat. The HTML version at `site/index.html` works in any browser — no installation, no account, nothing phones home.

## A note on tone

This guide is written like a friend explaining something over coffee. No jargon without a plain-English translation. No condescension. If a step feels obvious, skip it — you're the expert on your own kitchen.

## Medical disclaimer

This guide is for education and general meal-planning ideas only. It is not medical advice, nutrition counseling, or a substitute for care from a registered dietitian or your healthcare team. If you have a medical condition that affects what you can eat (diabetes, kidney disease, food allergies, pregnancy, etc.), talk to your doctor or dietitian before making big changes to your diet.
